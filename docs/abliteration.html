Abliterator · abliteration and sound design

What is Abliteration?

Abliterator takes its name from abliteration: removing a direction from a model's internal vectors so that it stops doing something. Here the word covers every edit to those vectors while the model plays, and the model is one that writes sound. This page says what that means for sound design, with real renders, their spectrograms, and comparisons with the audio effect that comes nearest.

01

The word

In the interpretability literature, abliteration means removing a refusal direction from a chat model. Arditi et al. (2024) found that refusal in a range of open chat models runs through a single direction in the residual stream: add it and a model refuses harmless requests, take it out and it stops refusing. Removing that direction from the model's weights, so that it no longer refuses, came to be called abliteration in the open-model community (Labonne 2024).

A music model has no refusals. What it has are directions for qualities of sound. Abliterator makes two from pairs of prompts that differ in one quality, “bright airy ambient pad, high shimmering tones” against the same piece asked for dark, low and muffled: brightness, and in the same way pulse. Removing one of those directions from what the model's blocks write is abliteration in the first sense, applied to timbre. Most of the other things the instrument does to the model remove no direction at all: they push the stream along one, turn it, squash it, quantise it, or feed it frames from the past.

So Abliterator uses the word in an extended sense, for any change to a generative model's internal vectors made while it runs. Terence Broad and his co-authors call this family active divergence, and its most direct form network bending: transformations inserted into a trained network as it generates (Broad, Leymarie and Grierson 2021; Broad, Berns, Colton and Grierson 2021). Abliterator calls its own form of this vector bending, since what it changes are the vectors the model computes with; abliteration is one kind of vector bending among many.

Add, remove, turn

Three things a unit can do along one direction of the stream. Steer adds it, Abliterate removes it, Rotate turns it toward another. Abliterate on this instrument

Abliterate takes a direction out of what each block writes, so a sound moves toward the middle of that axis rather than toward one end of it. How far it moves depends on how far along the axis the sound was. The Warm drone sits a little to the dark side of Brightness; with Brightness removed at every block its spectral centroid rose from 138 to 191 Hz. Deep techno sits far along Pulse; with Pulse removed it rose from 69 to 902 Hz and the level fell 8 dB. Where the unit acts also matters: along both directions almost all the writing is done in block 0 and blocks 9 to 11, so a strip on a single block in between changes little.

Above a strength of 1 the direction is turned round rather than removed: at 2 the sound goes as far the other way as it was, and this instrument lets Strength go to 6 (VAU stops at 1.5). On the Warm drone the centroid reached 627 Hz at 2 and 903 Hz at 3; on Deep techno, strengths of 4 and 6 made it noisier and sharper in its attacks. The ▾ menu on the strip holds five starting points, from Remove · 1 to Far side · 6, with VAU's own gentler settings as Late blocks · VAU; each keeps the direction you chose.

03

What it means for sound design

Sound design usually starts from a signal. An oscillator, a sample or a recording supplies it, and filters and effects shape it. Here there is no signal until the model writes one, 40 ms at a time, and the controls act on the state the model writes it from. A change there changes what is played: its register, its density, the instruments it seems to use, as well as its spectrum.

The examples below set the two side by side. In each, the first clip is the model as it plays; the second is an ordinary audio effect on that same clip; the third is the model with vector bending toward the same end.

Brighter

An EQ can brighten a sound only by raising what is already there. The Warm drone has little above a few hundred hertz, so a high shelf of +12 dB moves its spectral centroid from 137 Hz to 153 Hz and lifts its faint upper partials and noise. A Steer toward brightness of 0.2 at layer 6 moves the centroid to 1.76 kHz: the model writes higher material, and in the spectrogram the low drone below about 200 Hz all but goes.

Echo

A delay on the audio repeats the sound 160 ms later at a fifth of its level. Lookback adds the stream from four frames back (160 ms) to the stream at layer 6, with the same feedback and mix. The model then writes from that mixed state: on this clip it plays 8.6 dB quieter than without it, and at a mix of 0.4 and above it falls almost silent.

Depth

An audio effect has one place to go: after the sound. A unit has twelve, between the model's transformer layers, and the same unit at different depths does different things. Tanh Squash at its defaults broke the sound at layers 2 and 4 and held from 6 on. Choose a layer.

Movement from inside

Modulation moves the model's controls as it plays. In this saved sound LFO 1, a 0.2 Hz sine, swings the brightness dose, and LFO 2, a 0.5 Hz triangle, swings the sampler's temperature, over twenty seconds of the Warm drone. In the spectrogram the high band opens four times, once every five seconds, with LFO 1.

The edge as material

The knobs keep to the ranges where the measurements found the sound held. Past them, the sound breaks in ways no audio effect produces, and some of that is usable. Full range on the Steer card unlocks the doses; the units' knobs mark the settings that broke with ⚠.

Takes and recall

A seed is a take. The same seed, preset, settings and moves give the same sound, sample for sample, and a saved sound keeps all of them in its recipe, from which the engine renders it again exactly. Changing the seed changes the music about as much as the sampler's own knobs do: across seeds the spectrum moves about 8 dB. Auditioning seeds is part of the instrument, and so is fixing one.

04

Where the comparison with audio effects fails

Breaks

A direction is not a frequency band. Brightness is a direction made from prompts; it moves the spectrum, and it also moves register and instrumentation. Nothing orders the directions the way frequency is ordered.

Breaks

The output feeds the input. The model reads every frame it has written. A change at one frame carries into the frames after it, so a unit's effect builds and wanders, and switching it off does not return the sound to where it was.

Breaks

Time is frames. A model-layer unit's time is counted in 40 ms frames of the stream. Lookback's four frames are 160 ms of position, and what comes back is a state the model writes from, not a copy of the sound.

Breaks

Two numbers do not describe a sound. The measures here (level, spectral centroid, flatness, a long-term spectrum) find where a sound breaks. Whether a setting is musical is decided by ear, in the Tune view.
